## Let's start with a couple of assumptions: {#let-s-start-with-a-couple-of-assumptions}

1.  I watch a TV show, read a blog post or other website, watch a video, etc for the _**content**_ that show, website, blog post, video and so on provides. The _only_ reason I am visiting that site is for its content.
2.  I fully understand that it costs money to create and host that content, regardless of its medium, and that the creators' primary motivation (in most but not all cases) is to generate revenue. Therefore, I understand that advertisements are a necessary evil. For decades, advertising was what made over the air television profitable and that was fine: it made that entertainment free to me.


## Okay, So What's the Problem? {#okay-so-what-s-the-problem}

It is supremely frustrating to go to a webpage to read an article only to be met with a host of ads, requests to sign up for their newsletter, or to join the site in order to read the article.  John Gruber [outlined the problem](https://daringfireball.net/2026/03/your_frustration_is_the_product) clearly. As he notes: "We’re visiting their website to read a fucking article. " But we can't; they've covered it over with ads.

Go to just about any webpage and you'll find that there are pop-over ads that are unrelated to the article. You'll find autoplay videos, many of which overlay the article, You''ll find the _same ad_ repeated every other paragraph through the whole article. And on the saga goes. Now, on a standard computer, you can at least (usually) still see the article in order to read it, however frustrating the process may be. On a smaller screen, such as an iPhone or iPad, forget it.  Damn near the entire screen is covered over with ads. And, if you close the ad, it'll just pop back up a few moments later.
